Frequently asked questions

What is the hydroperiod?

The number of days a pixel holds surface water over one hydrological year. Rather than a single snapshot, it summarises a whole flooding cycle: how long the water stayed, not just whether it was there on the day a satellite happened to pass over.

Why does the hydrological year start on 1 September?

Because a calendar year would cut a flooding cycle in half. The cycle labelled 2021/2022 runs from 1 September 2021 to 1 September 2022. This also means the most recent cycle you can compute is not simply last year's: between January and August, the cycle that started last year is still open and its flooded days do not yet add up to a full year.

Which sensor should I choose?

It is a trade-off between resolution and how far back you can look:

  • Sentinel-2 — 10 m, from 2017. The best detail, the shortest record.
  • Landsat — 30 m, from 1984. The only option for long-term change.
  • MODIS — 500 m, from 2000. Coarse, but cheap over very large wetlands.

Which water index and threshold?

MNDWI is the sensible default and works across all three sensors. AWEI and its shadow-suppressing variant help where terrain shadow is confused with water; WI2015 is a tuned alternative. MODIS only offers MNDWI and NDWI.

The threshold is the value above which a pixel counts as water. Zero is the usual starting point; raise it if dry ground is being flagged as flooded, lower it if shallow water is being missed. It is worth checking a cycle you know well before trusting a whole series.

What do the layers mean?

  • Hydroperiod — flooded days in the cycle.
  • Normalized hydroperiod — the same, corrected for how many valid observations the pixel actually had.
  • Days with valid data — how many cloud-free observations backed the estimate. Low values mean the hydroperiod for that pixel is poorly constrained.
  • First / last flood day — when flooding started and ended, as day of year.
  • IRT — temporal regularity index: how consistently a pixel floods across the whole period, from 0 to 1. It summarises the period, so it does not belong to any single cycle.

What is the difference between the two anomaly references?

Mean of the selected period compares each cycle against the average of the years you asked for. Historical mean compares it against the sensor's entire archive. The first answers "was this year unusual for this period?"; the second, "was this year unusual, full stop?" — the historical option is considerably more expensive to compute.

Why does TWI ignore the sensor and the period?

Because it is derived purely from terrain: TWI = ln(a / tan β), where a is upstream drainage area per unit contour length and β the local slope. It describes where water tends to accumulate, not where it was. The 30 m hybrid option sharpens the slope using NASADEM, but flow accumulation still comes from MERIT at ~90 m — proper sink filling and D8 routing at 30 m is not practical on Earth Engine.

Why do I need my own Earth Engine account?

The computation runs on Google Earth Engine under your own credentials and quota, not a shared one. You need a Google Cloud project with the Earth Engine API enabled; you can register one here, or work through the manual, which covers it step by step. Your refresh token is stored encrypted and can be disconnected at any time.

The layer failed with a memory error. What now?

Earth Engine refused the computation because it did not fit. Shorten the year range, pick a coarser sensor, or work on a smaller wetland. Long periods over large wetlands at 10 m are the usual culprit.

Why does the layer only cover part of the wetland?

Hydroperiod products are masked to the pixels that held water at some point during the period. Ground that never flooded is left blank rather than drawn as zero.
